The Voice Behind The Flavours

From Student Politics to Uncovering Bharatpur’s Culinary Soul

"People outside thought Bharatpur was only about roadside kachoris. But having grown up here, I knew our heritage ran much deeper—into 70-year-old halwai recipes, slow-cooked royal game curries, and generational dhabas that have fed travellers for decades."

After student elections were paused at MSJ College where Rajat was actively preparing for presidency, he refused to sit idle. He picked up his microphone, stepped into the bustling bazaars, and founded Taste Safari. Today, millions watch his honest reviews and cultural storytelling.

The Taste Safari Creed

We never accept money to give false praise. If a dish is exceptional, we celebrate the hands that made it. If it isn't, we share constructive honesty. Taste Safari exists to document authentic heritage and support local culinary artisans.

Meet The Creator

Rajat Punia

Born and raised in Bharatpur, Rajasthan, Rajat was deeply involved in youth leadership and campus advocacy at MSJ College—the preeminent collegiate institution of the division. When regional student union elections were indefinitely postponed, he channeled his public speaking talent, curiosity, and love for his homeland into documenting its untold culinary heritage.

Bharatpur occupies a unique cultural crossroads where Rajasthani royal culinary finesse converges with Braj's sacred milk-sweet traditions and Uttar Pradesh's pungent mustard-heeng street food. Rajat recognized that while big cities get endless media coverage, the multigenerational artisans of Bharatpur were going unnoticed.
